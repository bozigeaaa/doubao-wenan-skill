Qiddiya Investment Company has awarded a contract worth as much as $700 million to build a major esports arena outside Riyadh, advancing one of the centrepieces of Saudi Arabia’s push to establish itself as a global gaming hub.

The contract was awarded to Beijing-based Metallurgical Corporation of China, according to MEED. Known as Fortress Arena, the project includes an auditorium with about 5,100 seats, alongside commercial areas, hospitality facilities and associated infrastructure.

Read more: [Chinese Firm Lands Qiddiya Cultural District Contract](https://www.constructionweeksaudi.com/projects-tenders/chinese-firm-lands-qiddiya-cultural-district-contract)

Located in Qiddiya City’s Gaming and Esports District, the arena is designed to host local and international competitions. Completion is expected by April 2029, with US-based Populous serving as architect and Thornton Tomasetti supporting the design.

China’s MCC is already active elsewhere in Saudi Arabia’s construction pipeline. The Chinese contractor has worked on the steel structure for Qiddiya’s Prince Mohammed bin Salman Stadium and has also been involved in construction of Ceer’s electric vehicle manufacturing complex at King Abdullah Economic City near Jeddah.

Read more: [El-Seif Wins Qiddiya F1 Viewing Lounge Contract](https://www.constructionweeksaudi.com/projects-tenders/el-seif-wins-qiddiya-f1-contract)

Gaming has become a major pillar of Qiddiya’s development and Saudi Arabia’s wider diversification strategy. The district is planned with four esports arenas and 73,000 seats across its venues, alongside space for gaming companies and esports clubs. Qiddiya says the destination is intended to attract as many as 10 million visits annually.

The esports arena is advancing alongside a wider pipeline of major sports venues across Qiddiya City, including the Speed Park Track, which is planned to host Formula 1, the [Prince Mohammed bin Salman Stadium](https://www.constructionweeksaudi.com/awards/qiddiya-221-million-2034-world-cup-stadium-contract) for the 2034 FIFA World Cup, a new National Tennis Centre and a 21,000-seat horse racing venue that will become the permanent home of the Saudi Cup.
