L478: Six groups vie for Qassim airport
L479: Six bidders have qualified to compete for the redevelopment of Prince Naif Bin Abdulaziz International Airport in Qassim, the Civil Aviation Holding Company (Matarat) cite130†said on X†x.com . The project covers a new passenger terminal and upgrades to runways, taxiways, aprons, and other airside infrastructure, to be built under a design-finance-construct-operate-maintain-transfer model. Of the cite131†89 companies that initially expressed interest, six consortia made the shortlist.
L480: The shortlist: Turkey’s YDA Insaat with Safari Group, Bahrain’s Lamar Holding, and France’s Egis; Saudi Arabia’s Ports Projects Management & Development with Algihaz Holding; Mada International Holding with Turkey’s TAV Airports; Namaya International Investment with Oman Airports, AlBawani Capital, and the UAE’s Tanama; Vision Invest with Asyad Holding and Ireland’s Daa International; and India’s GMR Airports.
L481: The wider push: Tendering is also underway for the new Taif International Airport, planned for 2.5 mn passengers by 2030 and built to handle demand through 2055. Both Qassim and Taif were awarded as PPP projects back in 2017, but those agreements stalled during Saudi Arabia’s aviation-sector restructuring, so this is the second time around for both, according to cite132†Meed†www.meed.com .

L487: Tasnee buys time on debt
L488: Tasnee has rescheduled an SAR 2 bn financing facility with Banque Saudi Fransi, extending the repayment profile to better match the company’s cashflows, according to a cite135†Tadawul disclosure†www.saudiexchange.sa . The facility, originally signed in January 2019, has been fully rescheduled with a seven-year tenor, unchanged from the original term. The financing is backed by promissory notes.
L489: Why it matters: The rescheduling is another step in Tasnee’s broader balance-sheet cleanup. Tasnee sold Al Rowad for SAR 684 mn to cite136†exit plastics conversion and redirect capital toward debt reduction. An SAR 889 mn cite137†net loss†www.saudiexchange.sa in 1H, versus SAR 830 mn in net income a year earlier, has added to the pressure. The rescheduling gives Tasnee more room to manage its debt while it refocuses on upstream petrochemicals.

L491: FlyNow plots Saudi eVTOL plant
L492: FlyNow Arabia plans to build a mass-production facility in Saudi Arabia, following the relocation of its headquarters to Riyadh, CEO Yvonne Winter told Asharq Business (cite138†watch, runtime 3:18†asharqbusiness.com ). The eVTOL maker is developing the plant with support from the General Authority of Civil Aviation and recently signed MoUs covering c. 700 aircraft for various uses.
L493: The timeline: FlyNow expects to start cargo and firefighting services after a roughly two-year pilot phase, then launch air taxi services by 2030, timed to Expo. The rollout runs in sequence: testing first, then logistics and firefighting, then passenger transport.
L494: REMEMBER- FlyNow announced its expansion plan in 2025, including setting up an cite139†eCopters production facility and introducing the Kingdom’s cite140†first flying taxi†enterprise.news . It also ran sandbox flights last year with local and international partners to demonstrate safe eVTOL integration in low-altitude airspace.
